For engineering and manufacturing teams

Designed experiments that get more information from fewer runs.

Design of experiments consulting for engineering and manufacturing: factorial and fractional-factorial designs, screening experiments, process improvement, and quality methods.

Every experimental run costs something: machine time, material, or a day of production. Design of experiments is how you make each run answer several questions at once. It is my main research area.

Typical questions

What people usually ask me

  • We have ten factors that might matter. How do we find the few that do?
  • Is changing one setting at a time good enough?
  • How many runs do we need, and in what order?
  • Our factors have a natural default setting. Does that change the design?
  • How do we test software or a system with many configuration options?

How I help

What the work looks like

  • Screening: fractional-factorial designs that sort the important factors from the rest in few runs.
  • Optimization: follow-up designs to find the best settings for the factors that matter.
  • Designs with a baseline: when each factor has a default level, designs under the baseline parameterization estimate effects relative to that default directly. Most of my published papers are on these designs.
  • Testing: covering arrays and related designs for testing systems with many settings.
  • Training: design of experiments workshops for engineers, built around your own processes where possible.

Example

From past work

UFV’s Automation and Robotics program needed a statistics course aligned to technologist certification. I advised the design of Statistics for Electronics (control charting, acceptance sampling, regression, and experimental design), mapped to what the certification examines.
